What a Solar Setup Actually Needs

Every solar charging setup, from a windowsill rig to a full rooftop array, is built from the same five pieces. Here's what each one does — and which ones I actually had to buy.

Solar Panel

Captures sunlight and converts it into raw DC electricity. This is the only piece that has to sit outside in the sun.

Charge Controller

Regulates the voltage and current coming off the panel so it doesn't overcharge, overheat, or damage the battery cells.

Battery Bank

Stores the energy for later use. The Grid Doctor's 320Wh LiFePO4 pack is exactly this — it's the "battery" in the box.

Inverter

Converts stored DC power into AC, which is what's needed to run standard wall-outlet devices instead of just DC ports.

BMS (Battery Management System)

Watches over the cells — balances charge across them, and cuts power if there's overcurrent, overheat, or a short.

Wiring & Connectors

The cable and connector that carries power from the panel to the battery's charging port. Everything downstream is internal.

In a from-scratch DIY solar setup, the charge controller, inverter, and BMS are each separate components you'd have to source, wire, and fuse yourself. A power station like the Grid Doctor 300 folds all three into one sealed box.

The Plan

Add a panel. Charge a battery. Save the planet. Right?

Set the panel out whenever there's usable sun and let it feed the Grid Doctor.
Log every solar charge — date and how full the battery got.
Add it up in kWh at the end of the tracking period.
Apply Minnesota's grid factor (~0.4 kg CO₂ per kWh) to see what would've been burned instead.

1 panel → 1 house

A typical residential array is about 20–25 of my panels wired together, generating roughly 7,000–10,000 kWh a year instead of my 15–20. Same sunlight, same physics, same math — just scaled up to cover a whole home's draw instead of a phone.

So What

One panel is a starting point, not the finish line.

The same idea, sized up

My one panel proves the concept on a windowsill scale. Wire together twenty of them on a roof, pointed at the same sun, and you're offsetting a whole household's electricity instead of a phone charger — thousands of kilograms of CO₂ a year instead of single digits. The physics doesn't change between the two. Only the scale does, and scale is exactly the lever worth pulling next.
